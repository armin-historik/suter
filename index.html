Eugène F. Suter

Permanent-wave technology, international enterprise and a contested history

OverviewBiographyEugène MethodPatentsCompanyNessler & SuterDocumentsSources

Eugène François Suter (1887–1943) belonged to the generation of hairdressers and entrepreneurs who transformed electrically heated permanent waving into an international salon business. His career connects Swiss family history, London’s hairdressing trade, technical collaboration and the commercial competition that followed Charles Nessler’s pioneering work.

A history of people, machines and companies

Suter’s contribution cannot be reduced to a single machine or a single date. The historical record includes a biographical account from Horgen, patents covering specific technical arrangements, descriptions of the Eugène method and evidence of a company that continued to develop after its founder’s death. This dossier treats those different forms of evidence separately.

Why Suter matters

The emergence of the modern permanent wave involved multiple inventions and business models. Nessler’s earlier work, Suter’s commercial and technical development, Calvete’s electrical expertise and Mayer’s different winding approach belong to the same evolving field, but should not be collapsed into one invention story.

One especially valuable lead is the reported relationship between Suter and Nessler. A retrospective Horgen article describes Suter as an early employee of Nessler; that claim remains to be tested against contemporary records. A documented legal dispute between their companies provides a separate line of inquiry.

Research approach. Confirmed patent records are distinguished from retrospective biographical claims. Missing evidence is identified rather than replaced with certainty.
