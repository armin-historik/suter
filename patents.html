Patents provide the most concrete dated technical material currently assembled for this dossier. They show specific claimed devices and methods, but a patent’s title alone does not explain the complete commercial machine or establish who first invented permanent waving.

How to read these documents

Each patent needs five separate entries: named inventor or inventors, applicant or assignee where given, filing date, grant date and the precise subject of the claims. The drawings are invaluable for understanding arrangements, but the legal scope is defined by the claims, not by the illustration or the product’s advertising name.

The assertion that an “Eugène System” was patented in 1923 remains unconfirmed until an exact patent number and jurisdiction are established. A machine illustrated as manufactured in 1923 is not proof of a 1923 patent.
