The Eugène Company

From a London salon system to an international professional brand

OverviewBiographyEugène MethodPatentsCompanyNessler & SuterDocumentsSources

Eugène was more than the name of an apparatus. The brand encompassed equipment, professional preparations, salon practice and a network of hairdressers. Reconstructing the company’s history requires attention to products, trade publications and the distinction between Suter’s lifetime and the business’s later development.

Equipment and associated products

A technical history in the research collection describes Eugène Ltd selling heating equipment, curlers, setting lotions, shampoos, colour rinses and steaming sachets. It also mentions a London factory on Edgware Road. These are useful research leads, but precise addresses, product dates and company structures should be checked against trade directories, advertisements and company records.

Working with Calvete and Icall

The relationship with Isidoro Calvete is central to the manufacturing story. The technical account describes Calvete supplying apparatus marketed under the Eugène name while developing his own Icall equipment. This raises questions about manufacture, branding, patents and later competition that cannot be resolved by treating “Eugène” as the inventor of every component.

The Eugène Waver

The company published The Eugène Waver, a trade magazine combining professional advice, products and salon culture. The National Art Library at the Victoria and Albert Museum holds volumes covering 1928–1947. These issues may allow the history of the business to be traced through its own contemporary language, images and advertisements.

Read the V&A account of The Eugène Waver.

International markets and the Nessler competition

Eugène products entered an international market in which Nestlé-branded permanent-wave equipment was already established. The relationship was not only commercial: a United States patent dispute between Nestlé-LeMur and Eugene Limited provides contemporary legal evidence of competing technical claims. The specific parties, claims and outcome should be described from the court decision, not from later summaries.

After Suter’s death: the Gallia connection

Later accounts place the acquisition of the French permanent-wave equipment business Gallia by the Eugène company in 1955, twelve years after Suter’s death. The date and corporate transaction require confirmation from company or contemporary trade records. Gallia’s earlier history and the subsequent Eugène Gallia business will be treated in a separate dossier at gallia.hairdressing-history.org when that project is published.

This is a history of the company as well as its founder. Events after 1943 must not be attributed to Suter personally.
